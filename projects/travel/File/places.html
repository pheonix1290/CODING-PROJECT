Best Time to Visit

Kenya can be visited throughout much of the year, but different seasons offer different experiences

☀️ January – March — Hot & Mostly Dry

Weather: Warm to hot, generally dry, with
temperatures varying considerably by location.
Coastal areas tend to be warmer and more humid,
while higher areas can be cooler.

    What you can experiences:

  • Excellent conditions for safari and wildlife viewing

  • Clearer skies for photography

  • Beach holidays along the Kenyan coast

  • Hiking and outdoor activities

Good for:🦁 Safari | 🏖️ Beaches | 📸 Photography | 🥾 Outdoor adventures
🌧️ April – June — Long Rainy Season

Weather:This is Kenya's main long-rain period.
Rain can make some safari roads more difficult to
travel, particularly in parks and reserves.

What you can experience:

  • Green, vibrant landscapes

  • Waterfalls and fuller rivers

  • Fewer tourists in some destinations

  • Beautiful scenery for photography

  • Excellent opportunities for birdwatching

Good for:🌿 Landscapes | 🐦 Birdwatching | 📸 Photography | 💰 Quieter travel
🌦️ November – December — Short Rains & Green Landscapes

Weather:Kenya experiences another, generally
shorter rainy period around November and into December.
The rain can transform previously dry landscapes into
greener environments.

What u can experience:

  • Fresh green scenery

  • Wildlife enjoying newly available vegetation

  • Excellent birdwatching opportunities

  • Fewer visitors than during some peak periods

  • Christmas and holiday travel along the coast

Did u know ?

November can also be particularly interesting for birdwatchers
because migratory birds pass through Kenya around the rainy seasons

Good for🐦 Birdwatching | 🌿 Nature | 📸 Photography | 🏖️ Coastal holidays
🌤️ July – October — Cool/Dry & Popular

Wethaer:Generally cooler and drier, particularly compared
with the hotter beginning of the year. These months are among
the popular periods for visiting Kenya's parks

What you can experience:

  • Excellent wildlife viewing

  • Safari drives across relatively dry landscapes

  • The famous Great Migration period in the Maasai Mara

  • Cooler mornings and evenings

  • Great conditions for exploring national parks

Did u know ?

The Great Migration is one of Kenya's major wildlife attractions,
with wildebeest moving northwards towards the Maasai Mara during
the dry-season period

Good for:🦁 Safari | 🦓 Great Migration | 📷 Wildlife photography | 🏞️ National parks

Currency

Kenya uses the Kenyan shilling (KES).

It is useful to carry some local currency, although cards and digital payments are accepted in many hotels, restaurants and businesses.

Language

Kenya's official languages are English and Swhahili

You will hear both languages used throughout the country.

A few useful Swahili phrases:

  • Jambo– Hello
  • Asante– Thank you
  • Karibu– Welcome
  • Habari?– Welcome
  • Kwaheri-Goodbye

Getting around

Kenya has several ways to travel around the country.

You can use:

  • Domestic flights

  • Train

  • Buses

  • Taxis

  • Domestic flights

  • Tour vehicles

  • Private cars

For safari destinations, organised tours and safari vehicles are often convenient options.

What to Pack

  • Depending on your itinerary, consider bringing:

    • Comfortable walking shoes

    • Lightweight Clothing

    • A ligt jacket

    • Sun protection

      • Sunscreen(broad spectrum sunscreen (optional))
      • Sunhat
      • UV sunglasses(UV400 or 100% UV protection)
      • UPF clothing
    • Sunglasses
    • Reusable water bottle
    • Camera
    • Insect repellent
    • Personal medication(If needed)
    • Travel documents
      • Valid passport(upto date)
      • National identity card(if needed)
      • Visas or travel authorisations
      • Driver's licence or International drivers permit(IPD)

    Respect local Culture

    Kenya is home to many different communities and cultural traditions.

    When visiting villages, cultural sites or religious locations, be respectful. Ask permission before photographing people and follow local customs.

    Wild Life Safety

    • When visiting villages, cultural sites or religious locations, be respectful.

    • Ask permission before photographing people

    • Follow local customs

    Wild animals are not pets, and visitors should maintain a safe distance and avoid attempting to feed or approach animals.
